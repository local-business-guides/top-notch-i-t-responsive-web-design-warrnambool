Scope, schedule and terms to know

The project usually starts with a conversation about your business, your customers and what the website needs to do. From there, the pages, content and features can be considered before design and build work begins. Timing depends on the size of the site, how much content is ready and whether extra functions are required. There is no fixed schedule that fits every project.

Cost is shaped by the scope rather than one standard package. The number of pages, custom design requirements, content preparation, added functionality and ongoing services such as hosting or maintenance can all affect the work involved. Ask providers to explain what is included and what ongoing costs or tasks sit outside the initial project.

When comparing providers, look at examples of their work, how they handle mobile layouts, what you will be able to update yourself and what support is available after launch. Check that the proposed pages match your actual services, not just a visual template.

A few common terms:
- WordPress: a system used to build and manage website content.
- Hosting: the service that stores a website so people can access it online.
- SEO: work that helps search engines understand a site and its pages.
- Maintenance: ongoing care such as updates and checks.
